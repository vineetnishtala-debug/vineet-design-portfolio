The Canopy
Loop

A shared design space where making, repair, rest, and urban growing coexist beneath one continuous structure.

01 / Concept

One loop,
many rhythms.

The Canopy Loop asks what a design studio could become if focused work, hands-on repair, growing, and rest were treated as equally valuable parts of a creative practice.

The intended experience is a gradual shift between energy levels. Visitors can move from the social commons to shared work surfaces, then toward quieter pods and planted edges without ever feeling cut off from the larger community.

02 / Design principles

A space organized by behavior—not walls.

01

Keep sightlines open

Low furniture and a shared canopy make each activity visible and invite movement between zones.

02

Give each rhythm a place

Active making sits apart from quieter reflection while remaining part of one connected environment.

03

Use nature as structure

Planting is not decoration; it marks transitions, softens the interior, and supports the restorative edge.

03 / Spatial sequence

Follow the loop.

Five views test how the environment reads from overview to human scale.

04 / Refinement

From a room of objects to a legible experience.

The earlier version established the mood and key activities, but the final refinement needed a clearer hierarchy. I treated the central loop as the organizing gesture, strengthened the identity of each zone, and used multiple camera studies to evaluate circulation and visibility.

BeforeActivities read as separate elements placed in one room.
AfterThe canopy, path, and commons connect them as one spatial system.
TestedOverhead, reverse, zone-level, and eye-level views expose weak transitions.
ResolvedColor, scale, furniture, and planting now signal shifts in pace and use.
05 / Reflection

What I learned

The most important revision was not adding more detail—it was making the main idea easier to read. The continuous loop became a useful constraint: every zone had to contribute to the whole while retaining a distinct rhythm. The final result is more coherent, though future development would include accessibility studies, material tests, and feedback from people using the space.

AI + asset acknowledgment

How the work was made

Generative AI was used as an ideation partner for written prompts and early visual directions. I selected the concept, defined the program and spatial relationships, modeled and arranged the environment in Blender, chose the camera views, and made the final aesthetic and presentation decisions.

All displayed environment images and project documentation were created for this project. No external stock assets are presented as original work.
