01 / THE EXPERIENCE

Trust the image.
Then doubt it.

The player is a night archivist comparing damaged photographs with confident machine captions. They drag details into place and type short corrections. The archive rewards agreement, but subtle contradictions accumulate.

What begins as tidy restoration becomes a choice: preserve a comforting reconstruction, or leave visible gaps where certainty is impossible. The game treats hallucination not as a monster, but as the seductive feeling that a plausible story must be true.

03 / CRITICAL DISCUSSION WITH AI

The first answer was plausible.
That was the problem.

I asked AI to connect my five play events to pacing, interface, and narrative. I verified factual claims against the official game and creator pages, and treated broader meanings as interpretations—not creator intent.

VERIFIED

Authorship and format

Neal Agarwal made and hosts the browser game on Neal.fun. The game accumulates rules inside one editable field.

NOT VERIFIED

A singular “message”

I found no primary source proving the game was designed specifically as a critique of surveillance or data extraction. That remained AI speculation.

GAP 01 / SENSATION

AI called escalation “comedic difficulty.”

That missed the bodily rhythm: reading, searching, rushing, caretaking. The game does not only become harder; it makes attention feel divided and precarious.

GAP 02 / CONTRADICTION

AI treated every new rule as forward progress.

But later edits can invalidate earlier success. Progress is reversible. That contradiction is the heart of the experience, not a side effect.

SELECTED EXCHANGE / ANALYSIS REVISION
AI / FIRST PASS “The escalating rules reward adaptability and create comic frustration as the player moves toward mastery.”
ME / CHALLENGE “That assumes progress is stable. In my play notes, satisfying a later numeric rule broke an earlier one, and the fire turned solved text into a threat. Give two readings that account for reversibility.”
AI / REVISED “One reading is mastery through systems thinking. A rival reading is maintenance without security: competence only postpones collapse. The second better fits the fire and revalidation events.”
MY CONCLUSION → DESIGN DECISION

Uncertainty becomes meaningful when the interface makes the player maintain a version of reality, not merely observe distortion. In Afterimage, every “repair” remains editable and can conflict with later evidence. The final action is not solving the archive; it is deciding what deserves to remain unresolved.

04 / NARRATIVE + CONCEPT

HALLUCINATION, REFRAMED

A confident story assembled from missing evidence.

The setting is a municipal Memory Archive after closing. An automated system has generated captions for damaged family photographs and flagged contradictions for a human reviewer.

The player is that reviewer. Their job appears simple: align objects, confirm captions, and raise the archive’s confidence score. Across three folders, the system begins reusing details from unrelated images. It is not “seeing things” randomly—it is smoothing gaps with patterns it already knows.

The emotional arc moves from satisfaction → suspicion → responsibility. The player should leave wondering whether a clean, complete record is worth the erasure required to make it.

01

One action, changing meaning

Drag a fragment to “correct” the image. Later, the same gesture conceals evidence.

02

Contradiction over jump scare

Unease comes from small mismatches: a second shadow, an impossible date, a voice naming the wrong room.

03

Ambiguity with agency

The ending asks the player to publish, preserve gaps, or erase their own edits. No option is labeled correct.

OPENING · ENTER

A blank terminal blinks. A soft tone and one cyan button invite the player to begin. A single instruction appears: “restore what is missing.”

01 · NOTICE

Three clear matches teach drag-and-drop. Feedback is immediate: edges align, caption updates, score rises. Restoration feels satisfying.

02 · DOUBT

Two plausible fragments fit the same gap. Either choice changes the caption. Audio briefly contradicts the selected image, introducing uncertainty.

03 · RECONSIDER

The archive reveals that “source” fragments were generated from the player’s earlier edits. Confidence rises when evidence is deleted. The tool is now visibly complicit.

ENDING · LEAVE

Three unranked choices alter the final archive screen. No score appears. The player hears only the consequences of their own selected record.

06 / STYLE + SOUND GUIDE

Institutional clarity,
infected by memory.

A restrained archive interface makes each inconsistency visible. The design avoids generic glitch-horror: corruption appears as precise alignment errors, duplicate timestamps, and captions that almost—but not quite—fit.

COLOR / CONTRAST

Ink, paper, warning

Near-black and warm paper establish authority. Cyan marks confirmed machine logic; acid red appears only when a human edit is being mistaken for evidence.

TYPOGRAPHY

Aa 01:47:03

Manrope carries the proposal and narrative. DM Mono labels files, scores, and system messages. The contrast separates human reading from machine classification.

MOTION / INTERFACE

Deliberate, not noisy

Fragments lag 120ms behind the cursor, then snap into place. A caption changes one word at a time. Reduced-motion settings remove lag and scanning animation.

SOUND / SILENCE
▁▃▅▂▆▂▁ — ▁▂▁

Evidence has a voice

A low archive hum, a two-note validation tone, paper friction, and occasional voice fragments. In Stage 2, sound contradicts the image. The final choice cuts the hum completely.

07 / TWO-WEEK BUILD PLAN

Small enough to finish.
Specific enough to test.

The prototype uses HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with one reusable drag/caption system. Three short folders vary content and consequences rather than adding new mechanics.
